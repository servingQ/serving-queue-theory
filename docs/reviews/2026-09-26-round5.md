## Verdict

**Minor revision.** v0.13 has the measurement its title promises: on a
short-context replay where the prefill queue is the only wait (per-rank
load 0.17–0.36, interarrival CV² 1.0–1.6, the pool gauge at 1–7 %, the
batch cap of 8 reached in under 2 % of samples), forcing
10 % of follow-ups to miss raises the number in the prefill stage by an
amount the bracket of Prop. 1 predicts, and the Markovian finite-source
price understates it 2–3x because it has no second moment. I reproduced
the table from the raw data. Three things stand between the draft and a
submission, all fixable from the data on disk in about two days: (1) the
bracket prices only the 243 forced turns, while the forced arm at 2.5 s
also caused 52 extra unforced misses (26 in the baseline, 78 in the
forced arm; recomputed) through the memory pool, and pricing those puts
the observed rise inside the bracket in both cases instead of "1 of 2";
(2) the four ranks are the only replicates the paper has, and their
spread (Delta L_P over lo from 0.86 to 1.55, recomputed) is hidden
behind two summed numbers in the abstract; (3) the strongest facts in the
data are not in the paper: the head-of-line term is 55–65 % of the
price, 46–57 % of the observed rise is borne by turns that did not miss,
and the finite-source wait is the closest predictor of the queueing time
(0.7–1.7x) while its price is the worst (2–3x under). Say those three
things and §4.3 carries the thesis on its own.

**Single biggest remaining weakness.** One seed, one forced share, and a
derived workload (9 copies of 37 source sessions, appends scaled by
0.44, outputs cut to four tokens, think times capped at 30 s), which the
paper does not describe as such. A hostile reviewer will read "a
short-context variant of the same trace" as a real trace. The fix is one
honest sentence and, when the server is free, one more seed.

## Remaining major issues (most severe first)

### 1. The bracket counts the forced misses only; the forced arm caused 52 more misses, and those explain the 9 % overshoot

Where: analyze_price.py 139–157 (the sum runs over `forced_turns`
only); main.tex 828–838 ("Forcing 10 % of follow-up turns to miss adds
163 to 243 misses in the window ... inside the bracket in 1 of 2
cases"); abstract 101–103; Table 20; research-plan §0 and testbed.md
attribute the overshoot to "the server's prefill time under concurrency
is 1.10–1.23x the E1 model, which the bracket does not include".

What the paper says: 243 forced misses at 2.5 s, Delta L_P = 2.05
against [1.56, 1.88], 9 % above the upper end.

What the data says (recomputed, window [90 s, 830 s] after the first
arrival, all ranks):

| class of follow-up turn | s25_base | s25_m10 | s35_base | s35_m10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| full hit | 2340 | 2266 | 1568 | 1560 |
| forced (nonce) | 246 (all hit in the baseline arm) | 243 (cached = 0 in every one) | 162 | 163 |
| unforced miss (cached = 0) | 26 | **78** | 0 | 0 |
| partial (0 < cached < 90 %) | 24 | 23 | 11 | 10 |

The nonce works: 0 of 243 forced turns had any cached tokens, and only 4
of the 78 unforced misses follow a forced turn immediately, so the
mechanism does not leak into the next turn. But the forced arm holds
more KV (the pre-nonce prefix stays cached until LRU takes it, and the
forced turn writes a fresh 6.9k-token copy), and 72 of the 78 unforced
misses follow a gap of at least 29 s, i.e. LRU evicted the sessions
parked at the trace's 30 s cap. Pricing the unforced misses with (4) in
the same way (their own S^hit, S^miss, the baseline's lambda, rho, W)
and taking the forced arm's sum less the baseline's adds +0.25 to lo at
2.5 s and +0.08 of load summed over ranks (recomputed), so the bracket
becomes about [1.80, 2.3] and the observed 2.05 is inside; at 3.5 s there are no unforced misses in either
arm and nothing changes. The L_P decomposition by class agrees: the
unforced misses contribute +0.245 of the +2.05 rise at 2.5 s.

The explanation the research plan gives does not survive being applied:
scaling every service time by the server's own prefill-time inflation
(1.16x baseline, 1.22x forced arm at 2.5 s; 1.10x / 1.15x at 3.5 s)
moves the brackets to [2.44, 3.20] and [0.95, 1.09] (recomputed), above
the observation in both cases, because that inflation is not extra
service in the FIFO sense (the next request's chunks overlap the
previous request's tail) and it also inflates rho, E[S²] and W. Do not
use it as a correction.

The good news: the extra misses are the feedback loop of §3.3 (a miss
holds more memory, which evicts more state, which causes more misses)
observed at small amplitude, in the very arm that measures the price.
The proposition prices "a fraction q_i of arrivals that turn from hits
into misses" and does not care whether the policy change forced them
directly or through the pool.

Fix: in analyze_price.py, price every follow-up miss of the forced arm
that the baseline does not have (forced plus unforced, net of the
baseline's own unforced misses), print the unforced count per arm in
Table 20, and rewrite 828–838 as "the change adds 163 and 243 forced
misses and, at 2.5 s, 52 misses more through the pool (26 to 78
unforced); priced together, the rise sits inside the bracket at both
loads". Then the abstract's "1.01 to 1.32 times the lower bound" becomes
a statement about the bracket, not about the lower end. Two hours.

### 2. The per-rank spread is the paper's only replicate and it is hidden

Where: Table 20 (summed over four ranks); abstract 101–103; contribution
3, 184–185; §6 889–890 ("single seeds").

What the paper says: two numbers, 1.01x and 1.32x the lower end.

What the data says (recomputed from the same per-rank rows that
price_s*.json already holds): Delta L_P / lo per rank is 1.22, 1.15,
0.86, 0.90 at 3.5 s (two ranks 10–14 % below the lower end, one 11 %
above the upper end, one inside) and 1.21, 1.21, 1.31, 1.55 at 2.5 s
(all four above the upper end by 1–31 %). The summed 1.01 at 3.5 s is
partly cancellation across ranks. The four ranks are pinned round-robin
with the same spacing, so they are four quasi-replicates of one seed,
and their spread, about ±15–20 %, is the uncertainty the paper should
state. It is also the honest reading of "inside the bracket": at 3.5 s
the bracket is 12 % wide and the rank-to-rank spread is 35 %.

Fix: add the per-rank rows to Table 20 (or a per-rank figure in App. D;
the data is in price_s*.json), state "per rank the rise is within
±X % of the bracket" in §4.3, and in the abstract replace the two ratios
by one sentence with that tolerance. One hour, plus the abstract.

### 3. The strongest findings are in the data and not in the paper

Where: §4.3 819–845; contribution 3; abstract.

What the data says (recomputed):

- Term split of the summed lower bound: own recompute / head-of-line /
  load = 0.31 / 0.41 / 0.03 at 3.5 s and 0.44 / 1.01 / 0.11 at 2.5 s, so
  the head-of-line term of (4) is 55–65 % of the price. The observed
  rise splits the same way: its service part (lambda' E[S'] − lambda
  E[S]) is 0.29 and 0.48, its wait part 0.46 and 1.57, i.e. the delay a
  forced miss adds to the turns behind it is 1.6–3.3x its own extra
  service. That is the thesis ("a miss delays every prefill queued
  behind it, a quadratic term that chunked prefill does not remove")
  measured, and the paper does not say it.
- Who pays: of the +0.75 rise at 3.5 s, +0.35 (46 %) is on turns that
  hit or were first turns (hit TTFT 0.48 → 0.61 s, first turns 0.57 →
  0.74 s); of the +2.05 at 2.5 s, +1.16 (57 %) is on turns that did not
  miss (hits 0.57 → 0.89 s, first turns 0.71 → 1.29 s). The externality
  is visible in the bystanders' TTFT.
- The finite-source wait of Prop. 2 (N = 3–7 per rank, Z = decode +
  think 6.9–8.3 s) is 0.7–1.7x the server's queueing time on every rank
  of the light arms (0.034 vs 0.048 s at 5 s ... 0.129 vs 0.107 s at
  2.5 s), the closest of the three predictions, and its utilisation rho_N
  (0.13–0.33) matches the E1 load (0.17–0.36) within 0.04, so Prop. 2(ii)
  is seen with both sides in the regime it assumes. The paper's E2b
  paragraph never mentions the finite-source wait, only its price.
- Replacing the PK W in (4) by the server's measured queueing time
  changes lo by 1–2 % (0.743 → 0.734, 1.558 → 1.524): the bracket does
  not depend on which wait feeds the third term here. Say so; it
  pre-empts the objection.

Fix: three sentences in §4.3 and one clause in contribution 3; a "terms"
column (own / HOL / load) in Table 20. One hour.

### 4. The wait comparison uses the denominator that flatters the formula

Where: main.tex 826–828 ("the PK wait of (3) is 1.3 to 1.6 times its
queueing time of 0.04–0.11 s"); contribution 3, 182–184 ("puts the PK
prediction within 1.3 to 1.6 times the observed wait"); Table 19 columns
W_obs and W_q.

What the data says (recomputed, light arms): PK / W_q(server queue
time) = 1.1–1.9 per rank (1.3–1.6 on run means, as printed); PK /
W_obs(TTFT − S of the cost model) = 0.4–0.7. The two denominators differ
by 0.08–0.13 s: the engine's prefill time is 1.08–1.17x E[S] (0.03–0.06 s)
and the client-observed TTFT exceeds queue + prefill by 0.04–0.07 s,
rising with load (0.043 s at 5 s spacing, 0.069 s at 2.5 s), which is a
frontend wait the engine's metrics do not see. So the formula overstates
the engine's queue by 30–60 % and understates the total delay beyond the
model by 30–60 %; the paper reports the first only. Both are defensible
readings; reporting one is not.

Fix: one sentence decomposing TTFT = frontend + queue + prefill with the
three measured pieces, then "PK is 1.3–1.6x the engine's queueing time
and 0.4–0.7x the residual TTFT − S". Contribution 3: "within a factor of
two of the observed wait on either reading". One hour.

### 5. The 1.5 s arm is attributed to the pool alone; the batch cap binds there

Where: main.tex 840–845 ("83 live sessions fill the pool's blocks, LRU
evicts suspended prefixes and the misses feed the load, the instability
of §3.3 on hardware"); testbed.md lists both mechanisms.

What the data says (recomputed from metrics.jsonl over the window):
`num_requests_running` reaches 8 (the cap) in 19–25 % of samples on
every rank and in 23–30 % of the samples with a request waiting; mean
ITL is 1.56 s (0.017 s idle; 0.09–0.44 s in the light arms), so a
four-token turn holds a running slot for seconds after its first token
while prefill chunks interleave; running averages 5.0–5.7. Natural
misses are 21.5 % of follow-ups and every one follows a gap of at least
29 s. TTFT rises 0.7 → 16 s over the run (non-stationary, as the paper
says). The pool gauge (19–22 %) counts running requests only and cannot
show cached blocks, so "fill the pool's blocks" is inferred from the
misses, not observed.

So the arm shows two things at once: the memory feedback of §3.3 (real,
via the 30 s-gap evictions) and a decode-slot bottleneck that the model
of §2.2 assumes away (chunked prefill does not protect decode on this
stack, §6). The sentence as written claims only the first. Note also
that in the light arms the cap is reached in 0.1–1.2 % of samples, which
is worth one clause because the E2 paragraph says "the batch cap never
binds" and a reader will ask about E2b.

Fix: "one session every 1.5 s saturates the replica by two routes: ...
the batch cap of eight binds in a quarter of the samples because decode
steps stall behind prefill chunks (ITL 1.6 s), and LRU evicts the
prefixes parked at the 30 s gap (22 % of follow-ups miss)". Half an
hour.
