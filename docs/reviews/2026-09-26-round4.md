## Verdict

**Major revision, for the right reason.** v0.11 is the first draft with
measurements, and the measurements are the most interesting thing in
the paper: on real hardware with 50k-token contexts the prefill queue
is nearly idle (rho <= 0.4), a turn whose prefix is fully cached starts
in 2.6 s regardless of load (recomputed), a turn whose prefix was
evicted waits 100 to 260 s for KV blocks, and capping live sessions at
8 halves the end-to-end session time even after the wait at the door
is counted (recomputed: 2583 s to 1344 s). None of that is in the paper
in that form. What is in the paper instead is (a) a hit/miss
classification that is a tautology in the code, so every hit-rate,
hit-TTFT and mixture-share number in §4.3, the abstract and
contribution 3 is wrong (the corrected numbers make the story
stronger); (b) a finite-source column that omits the decode phase from
the think time, so it prints a saturated closed server next to an
observed rho of 0.38, and visibly violates the paper's own Prop. 2(ii);
(c) a claim that the wait is for KV space that the evidence in the
paper does not establish and the evidence in the data does; (d) a cap
comparison that hides the admission wait, which the paper's own Table
8 caption says must be shown; and (e) a simulator decode constant taken
from the ITL of the most loaded run, when the ITL halves at cap 8.
These are analysis fixes on existing data, two days of work, no server
time. After them §4.3 will say less about Prop. 1 (it does not measure
the price where it matters, and should say so in one sentence) and
more about memory, which is where the data is.

**Single biggest remaining weakness.** The paper's central object,
Phi_i of (4), is still unmeasured: E2 as designed (forced misses at a
non-binding pool, rise in summed TTFT against the bracket) was not run,
because at 50k contexts the pool binds first. The section is honest
about this ("the open regime of (3) is not reached"), but the abstract
and contribution 3 present the miss/hit TTFT ratio as if it were the
price, and it is not: the miss TTFT is 90 % waiting for blocks, which
(4) does not contain.

### 1. The hit/miss classification is a tautology; every hit number in §4.3, the abstract and contribution 3 changes

Where: scripts/exp/analyze_e2.py line 147:
`is_hit = cached >= 0.9 * (pt - n) and cached > 0`, with `n = max(pt -
cached, 1)` two lines earlier, so `pt - n == cached` and the condition
is `cached > 0`. The docstring ("hit: cached >= 90 % of the prefix") and
the Table 18 caption ("follow-up turns whose cached prefix covers their
context", paper_e2_tables.py line 70) describe a test the code does not
perform. A follow-up turn with one sub-block of 50k still cached is a
"hit".

Recomputed with prefix = previous turn's prompt + completion tokens of
the same session (rounds.jsonl has both), follow-ups only:

| run | paper "hit" (cached > 0) | cached >= 90 % of prefix | partial (0 < cached < 90 %) | cached = 0 | mean TTFT full hit / partial / miss (s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| s = 20, cap inf | 51 % | 46 % | 4 % | 49 % | 2.6 / 80.1 / 278.6 |
| s = 20, cap 16 | 63 % | 59 % | 4 % | 37 % | 2.6 / 17.3 / 184.1 |
| s = 20, cap 8 | 95 % | 83 % | 12 % | 5 % | 2.6 / 24.5 / 117.3 |
| s = 10, cap 8 | 96 % | 84 % | 12 % | 4 % | 2.0 / 25.5 / 142.4 |

Consequences. (a) "TTFT of 3.5–9.1 s" for hits (main.tex 810–811,
`\eTwoHitTtftLo/Hi`) is the mean over full and partial hits; the full
hit's TTFT is 2.0–2.6 s in every run, which is P(4–5k, 50k) of (1)
almost exactly, i.e. a hit does not queue at all. That is a cleaner
finding than the one printed. (b) The miss/hit ratio in the abstract
(98–100) and contribution 3 (185–186), "22 to 53 times", becomes 45 to
107. (c) "the hit rate falls from 96 % under the tight cap to 51 % with
none" (812–813) becomes 84 % to 46 %; at cap 8, 12 % of follow-ups are
partial hits that re-prefill on average 31 % of their context, all of

cap 8; ranks 0–2: 0 % misses). (d) The "mix" column and
`\eTwoShareLo/Hi` (44–87 %, main.tex 829–831) are the between-class
variance of these labels and must be regenerated. (e) The per-rank
token-level reuse the server itself counts
(`vllm:prefix_cache_hits_total / queries_total`, already in

completion_tokens` as the prefix, classify hit / partial / miss with
the 90 % rule, report the three TTFT means and the token-level reuse;
regenerate. Then rewrite main.tex 809–815 as: "a follow-up turn whose
prefix is resident starts in [2.0–2.6] s, the service time of its
append, at every cap; one whose prefix was evicted waits [117–279] s,
of which the recompute is [16–20] s by (1) and the rest is the wait for

### 2. The finite-source column omits the decode phase and contradicts Proposition 2 in the table

Where: analyze_e2.py lines 173–176: Z is the gap from a turn's `done`
to the next `sent` (think time only, 6.3–8.7 s per rank) and the MVA is
run with that Z and E[S] from (1). Table 18 then prints finite (52.1 s)
above PK (3.2 s); Figure 1(c) draws the same; main.tex 819–822 reads it
as "closer than the open formula but below the observation ... since it
prices work and not memory".

Prop. 2(ii) says W_N <= the open wait at the same utilisation. The
column violates it because the two are not at the same utilisation:
recomputed per rank at cap inf, the MVA with Z = think gives rho_N =
0.96, 1.00, 1.00 on ranks 1–3 (W_N = 12.7, 25.9, 127.9 s) where the
observed rho is 0.14, 0.22, 0.38. The closed model attributes the whole
cycle time to the prefill server because its delay station omits the
decode phase (73–162 s per turn on this testbed; mean output 1.1k
tokens at an ITL of 0.06–0.12 s) and the wait for blocks. With Z =
think + decode the same recursion gives W_N = 1.3, 3.6, 36.6 s at rho_N
= 0.19, 0.37, 0.87, still above the observed utilisation because the
KV wait is still unmodelled. The "closer than the open formula"
sentence is an artefact of the wrong Z, and a reader who knows MVA
will see finite > PK in the table and stop trusting the section.

Fix (one of): (a) compute W_N at the observed utilisation (solve Z from
rho_N = rho_obs; then W_N <= PK by Prop. 2 and both are 10–100x below
the observation), and say so: "both compute-only predictions lie one to
two orders of magnitude below the observed queueing time"; or (b) use Z
= think + decode, label the column "finite source, delay = think +
decode", and drop "closer than the open formula". Either way delete
"since it prices work and not memory" as an explanation of a number
that was wrong for a different reason, and keep the conclusion the data
does support: no model of the prefill server explains the wait. Two
hours.

### 3. "The wait is for KV space" is asserted, not shown; the data can show it

Where: main.tex 815–819 ("the per-rank utilisation stays at 0.03–0.39
and the PK wait it implies is a few seconds, while the server's mean
queueing time is 6.8–122.6 s, spent waiting for KV space"); abstract
100–101; contribution 3 (186–187); §6 (904–905 "it bound in §4.3").

What the paper gives: rho low, PK small, W_q large, KV occupancy 37–61 %
(run average). A mean occupancy of 61 % is evidence against a binding
pool, not for it, and "not compute" does not imply "memory": the
sequence cap (8 per rank), the 512-token budget behind a running
prefill and the scheduler's admission rule are the alternatives a
reviewer will name.

What the data gives (recomputed from metrics.jsonl over the analysis
window, per rank): `num_requests_running` never exceeds 4 on any rank
in any run, so the sequence cap of 8 never binds; the pool is 52 blocks
of 4096 tokens (212,992 tokens per rank, serve log line 722), so a
50k-token miss needs 13 blocks and a hit's append about one; when a
request is waiting the mean occupancy is 0.79–0.81 (fewer than 13 free
blocks) with 3.0–3.2 running, and occupancy exceeds 0.9 in only 14–36 %
of the waiting samples. So the wait is for enough free blocks to admit
a whole context, and hits (one block) do not wait while misses (13
blocks) do, which is exactly the 2.6 s vs 117–279 s split of Major
issue 1. The server's `request_prefill_time` (12.1 s at cap inf, 4.1 s
at cap 8, already in summary.json as `pf_srv`) agrees with E[S] from
(1) within 8–20 % per rank, which is the "cost model under concurrency"
defence the paper needs and does not give.

Fix: add to §4.3 the pool size in blocks, "no rank ever ran more than
four sequences", the occupancy conditional on a request waiting, and
the server's prefill time next to E[S]; move the run-average occupancy
to the appendix table with that label. Then "the wait is for KV space"
is a demonstrated statement. Half a day, no runs.

### 4. The cap comparison omits the wait at the door; the strongest number in the section is missing

Where: main.tex 822–828 ("Third, the cap decides: at 8 live sessions
the pool holds almost every resident prefix and p99 TTFT falls from
503 s to 175 s (the admission sweep of Appendix B, measured)"); Table
18 has no entry-wait or throughput column; Figure 1 has none.

rounds.jsonl records `admission_wait_s` per session. Recomputed: at cap
16, 14 of 37 sessions waited at the door (mean 498 s, max 2215 s); at
cap 8, 25 of 37 (mean 636 s, max 2173 s); at cap 8 with 10 s spacing,
28 of 37 (mean 770 s, max 2407 s). Table 8's own caption says an entry
wait of hundreds of seconds must be shown because it means the replica
does not carry the offered rate. The paper cannot report p99 TTFT
falling under a cap and hide where the delay went.

The good news is that the number the paper should report survives:
mean session time in the system is 2583 / 1641 / 708 / 711 s across the
four runs, and with the door wait added 2583 / 2139 / 1344 / 1481 s;
the whole trace finishes in 5253 / 4929 / 4005 / 4017 s, i.e.
throughput rises from 0.070 to 0.092 turns/s under the cap because
thrashing wastes prefill. That is "the cap decides" with the
confounder removed, and it is the sentence a systems reviewer will
believe.

Fix: add columns "entry wait (s)" and "X (turns/s)" (or makespan) to
Table 18 and macros for the sojourn with and without the door wait;
rewrite 822–828 around them. Two hours.

### 5. The simulator's decode constant is the ITL of the most loaded run; the ITL halves at cap 8

Where: validation.rs 2083 (`CAL_DECODE_STEP = 0.125`, comment "E2
ITL"); simulation.tex 18–23 ("a decode iteration of omega = 0.125 s,
the mean inter-token latency of the E2 replays, which did not vary
with the context (beta = 0)"); research-plan §0.

Recomputed from rounds.jsonl (turns with >= 20 output tokens): mean ITL
0.122 s at cap inf, 0.110 s at cap 16, 0.057 s at cap 8, 0.063 s at cap
8 / 10 s; minimum 0.020 s; within the cap-inf run 0.159 s in the first
300 s and 0.118 s after. So omega is load-dependent by a factor of two,
and the "no context dependence" reading (0.137 s at 20–40k, 0.109 s at
60k+ within the cap-inf run) is confounded with run phase. The most
likely mechanism is the one the model assumes away: chunked prefill
does not protect decode on this stack (each 512-token chunk of a
co-scheduled miss lengthens every decode step), which is also a
limitation the paper should state, since §2.2 builds the two-stage
separation on it.

Fix: state omega as a range and which run calibrated it; either
calibrate on the least loaded run and treat the rise as the
interference the model omits, or report the sensitivity of Table 12 to
omega in {0.06, 0.12}; add one sentence to §6. Note that `make sim`
must be rerun if the constant changes. Half a day of CPU.

### 6. The abstract and contribution 3 call the TTFT ratio the price of a miss

Where: abstract 98–100 ("a follow-up miss costs 22 to 53 times a
hit"); contribution 3 (184–186, "a replay of a coding-agent trace shows
the follow-up miss costing 22 to 53 times the hit"); §4.3 809 ("First,
the price of a miss:"); Figure 1(a) title "the price of a miss".

The miss TTFT (117–279 s) is the recompute of (1) (16–20 s) plus a wait
for blocks (100–260 s). Phi_i of (4) contains neither the block wait
nor anything the memory pool does; it is the prefill-compute
externality, and E2 as designed (Table 19, App. E: forced misses, rise
in summed TTFT against the bracket) was not run. A reviewer who reads
§2.4 and then the abstract will conclude the measurement validates (4);
it does not, and §4.3 says so only in the last sentence.

Fix: abstract: "a turn whose prefix was evicted waits [45] to [107]
times as long as one whose prefix is resident, almost all of it for KV
blocks; the wait is for memory, not compute, and the cap on live
sessions decides it". Contribution 3: add "the price (4) itself is not
yet measured: the pool binds before the prefill queue does at these
context lengths". §4.3: retitle "First, the price of a miss" as "First,
what a miss costs here". One hour.

### 8. What a fourth reviewer will attack, and what to do about it now

  `prefill_time` comparison; print it.
- *Transient run.* The window is first-sent to last-done, 37 sessions

- *Sub-block granularity.* `cached_tokens` is rounded down to the

  90 % rule absorbs it; say so once.
- *Host-tensor mode.* docs/testbed.md argues TTFT is unaffected; one

- *The fourth run.* It shows that with the cap binding the arrival
