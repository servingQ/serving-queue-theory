\chapter{Metastability: When a Burst Outlives Itself}\label{sec:L7}
\input{metastability-tables}

\Cref{L6:sec:feedback} found the hit rates a replica can sustain as fixed points of a
feedback map, and \cref{L6:prop:collapse} showed that there can be two. A fixed point
does not say which equilibrium the replica occupies, how long it stays there, or what it
takes to leave. These are questions about dynamics. A \term{metastable failure} is
their bad answer. A temporary stressor, such as a burst of arrivals, moves the system
into a degraded state, and the system stays there after the stressor ends.
Alvaro et al.~\cite{alvaro2025} analyse such failures for request--response servers with timeouts and
retries. They build a continuous-time Markov chain from a program, draw its drift field,
and measure metastability by escape probabilities and by the eigenvalues of the
generator near zero. This lecture applies that programme to a prefill queue that loses
prefixes while it is congested. The stressor is the same. The mechanism is different,
and the difference changes the answers.

The feedback loop of serQ issue~\#120 is
\begin{align*}
  &\text{longer queue}\ \to\ \text{prefixes lost while waiting}\ \to\ \text{more recompute}\\
  &\quad\to\ \text{slower service}\ \to\ \text{longer queue}.
\end{align*}
We project it onto the queue length (\cref{L7:sec:model}), solve the resulting
birth--death chain exactly (\cref{L7:sec:modes,L7:sec:passage}), and obtain three
results. In the open queue, stability is decided by the miss work alone
(\cref{L7:sec:open}). In a closed population, the time to recover grows exponentially
with the population (\cref{L7:sec:closed}). And steady state and recovery rank
interventions differently (\cref{L7:sec:interventions}). \Cref{L7:sec:enough} then asks
whether the queue length is enough state, using a chain that also tracks the cache.

\section{The model}\label{L7:sec:model}

\begin{definition}[Queue with wait loss]\label{L7:def:model}
Turns join one prefill queue. When $n$ turns are present, the mean service time is
\[
  s(n)=S^{\mathrm{hit}}+m(n)\,\Delta S,\qquad \Delta S=S^{\mathrm{miss}}-S^{\mathrm{hit}}\ge0,
\]
where $m(n)\in[0,1]$ is the probability that a turn served at queue length $n$ has lost
its prefix. Service is exponential with rate $\mu_n=1/s(n)$. In the \term{open} queue,
turns arrive at rate $\lambda_n=\lambda$. In the \term{closed} queue, $N$ sessions each
think for an exponential time of mean $Z$ and then submit a turn, so
$\lambda_n=(N-n)/Z$.
\end{definition}

The function $m$ carries the wait channel of \cref{L6:sec:feedback}. A turn that finds a
long queue waits longer. While it waits, admissions ahead of it insert blocks, and an LRU
engine evicts the waiting prefix first, because its last use is older than that of any
session still thinking. So $m$ is nondecreasing. It is close to 0 while the queue fits
in the cache and tends to 1 once the queued prefixes cannot all be held. The shape of
$m$ is a property of the engine and the workload. It has to be measured. The theorems
below need only the stated monotonicity or limit.

\begin{remark}[The correspondence with retries]\label{L7:rem:alvaro}
In \cite{alvaro2025} a state is a pair $(u,v)$: $u$ requests in the server and its
queue, and $v$ requests in an \emph{orbit} waiting to be retried. The probability that a
request times out rises with $u$, and a timed-out request returns as extra
\emph{arrivals}. In our model a lost prefix returns as extra \emph{work}. The number of
turns is conserved, and the feedback acts on the service rate $\mu_n$, not on the
arrival rate. The role of the orbit is taken by the sessions and queued turns whose
prefix is gone. \Cref{L7:sec:enough} restores this second coordinate. Three consequences
follow from the change. The closed queue is a finite, irreducible chain, so it has a
unique stationary law, and metastability there means slow mixing, never instability. The
open queue's stability limit does not depend on where the losses start
(\cref{L7:thm:open}). And the projection onto the queue length is a birth--death chain,
whose passage times have closed forms.
\end{remark}

\section{Modes are stable fluid equilibria}\label{L7:sec:modes}

By \cref{thm:birthdeath}, the stationary law of a finite birth--death chain on
$\{0,\dots,M\}$ is $\pi(n)\propto w_n$, where $w_0=1$ and
$w_{n+1}=w_n\lambda_n/\mu_{n+1}$. Its shape follows from one comparison.

\begin{proposition}[Modes of the stationary law]\label{L7:prop:modes}
Let all rates be positive.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\pi(n+1)\ge\pi(n)$ if and only if $\lambda_n\ge\mu_{n+1}$.
\item A level $n+1$ is a local mode, $\pi(n)\le\pi(n+1)\ge\pi(n+2)$, if and only if
  $\lambda_n\ge\mu_{n+1}$ and $\lambda_{n+1}\le\mu_{n+2}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(i) $\pi(n+1)/\pi(n)=\lambda_n/\mu_{n+1}$. (ii) Apply (i) at $n$ and at $n+1$.
\end{proof}

The \term{fluid drift} at $n$ is $\lambda_n-\mu_n$. \Cref{L7:prop:modes} says that the
modes of $\pi$ are the levels where the drift changes from up to down, the stable
equilibria of the fluid model, and the troughs are the levels where it changes from
down to up, the saddles. In the closed queue the drift is
$(N-n)/Z-1/s(n)$. Its arrival term falls linearly in $n$, and its service term falls in
$n$ as well, steeply once $m$ rises. So the two can cross three times: a \emph{good} mode
with few losses, a saddle, and a \emph{bad} mode with nearly every queued turn a miss.
When they do, we call the law \term{bimodal}. The \term{barrier} from a mode $b$ down to
a saddle $k<b$ is $\Delta V=\log(\pi(b)/\pi(k))$.

\section{Passage times and recovery}\label{L7:sec:passage}

Let $T_k$ be the mean time for the chain on $\{0,\dots,M\}$ to go from $k$ to $k-1$, and
let the \term{recovery time} from $m$ to $n<m$ be $R(m,n)=\sum_{k=n+1}^{m}T_k$. By the
strong Markov property, a birth--death chain must cross every level in between, so
$R(m,n)$ is the mean hitting time of $n$ from $m$.

\begin{proposition}[Passage times of a birth--death chain]\label{L7:prop:passage}
Let all rates be positive. Then
\[
  T_k=\frac{1}{\mu_k}\sum_{j=k}^{M}\frac{\pi(j)}{\pi(k)}\qquad(1\le k\le M),
\]
and this is the unique solution of the first-step equations $\mu_MT_M=1$ and
$\mu_kT_k=1+\lambda_kT_{k+1}$ for $k<M$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
From $k<M$, the chain leaves after an exponential time of mean $1/(\lambda_k+\mu_k)$.
With probability $\mu_k/(\lambda_k+\mu_k)$ it steps down. Otherwise it steps up and
must first return to $k$, which takes $T_{k+1}$, and then start again. So
$T_k=\frac{1+\lambda_k(T_{k+1}+T_k)}{\lambda_k+\mu_k}$, which rearranges to
$\mu_kT_k=1+\lambda_kT_{k+1}$. At the top, $T_M=1/\mu_M$. These equations determine $T$ by
backward induction from $M$, so the solution is unique. Write $P_k=\sum_{j\ge k}\pi(j)$.
The formula gives $\mu_MT_M=1$. For $k<M$ it gives $\mu_kT_k=P_k/\pi(k)$, and by the cut
equation $\pi(k)\lambda_k=\pi(k+1)\mu_{k+1}$,
\[
  1+\lambda_kT_{k+1}=1+\frac{\lambda_kP_{k+1}}{\pi(k+1)\mu_{k+1}}
  =1+\frac{P_{k+1}}{\pi(k)}=\frac{P_k}{\pi(k)}. \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

The ratio $\pi(j)/\pi(k)=\prod_{i=k}^{j-1}\lambda_i/\mu_{i+1}$ involves only rates at
levels $\ge k$. Three consequences follow at once.

\begin{corollary}[Locality and monotonicity of recovery]\label{L7:cor:local}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $R(m,n)$ depends only on the rates at levels above $n$. Changing the service at
  levels $\le n$ leaves every recovery to $n$ unchanged.
\item If $\lambda'_i\ge\lambda_i$ and $\mu'_i\le\mu_i$ at every level $i>n$, then
  $R'(m,n)\ge R(m,n)$. Faster service or fewer arrivals above $n$ never lengthen the
  recovery.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
(i) is the remark above. (ii) Each factor $\lambda_i/\mu_{i+1}$ and the prefactor
$1/\mu_k$ is nonnegative and grows when $\lambda_i$ grows or $\mu_i$ shrinks.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Arrhenius lower bound]\label{L7:prop:arrhenius}
For every $j\ge k$, $T_k\ge\pi(j)/(\pi(k)\mu_k)$. In particular, if $k$ is a saddle and
$b>k$ a mode with barrier $\Delta V$, then the recovery past the saddle from any level
$\ge k$ takes at least $e^{\Delta V}/\mu_k$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Keep one term of the nonnegative sum in \cref{L7:prop:passage}.
\end{proof}

This is the lower half of the Arrhenius law. When the barrier is proportional to a
system size, the time to escape a well is exponential in that size, and
\cref{L7:sec:closed} shows this for the population $N$.

\section{The open queue: stability is set by the miss work}\label{L7:sec:open}

\begin{theorem}[Stability by d'Alembert's ratio test]\label{L7:thm:open}
In the open queue let $\lambda>0$, $s(n)>0$, and $s(n)\to S^{\mathrm{miss}}$ as
$n\to\infty$; for instance $m(n)\to1$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item If $\lambda S^{\mathrm{miss}}<1$ the queue has a stationary distribution. If
  $\lambda S^{\mathrm{miss}}>1$ it has none.
\item If $s'$ agrees with $s$ beyond some queue length $K$, then the queue with $s'$ has
  a stationary distribution if and only if the queue with $s$ does.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(i) By \cref{thm:birthdeath} a stationary distribution exists if and only if
$\sum_nw_n<\infty$, where $w_n=\prod_{k=1}^n\lambda s(k)$. The ratio
$w_{n+1}/w_n=\lambda s(n+1)$ tends to $\lambda S^{\mathrm{miss}}$. The ratio test gives
convergence when the limit is below 1 and divergence when it is above 1.
(ii) For $n\ge K$, $w'_n=(w'_K/w_K)\,w_n$, so the two tails differ by a positive
constant factor.
\end{proof}

The hit work $S^{\mathrm{hit}}$ and the shape of $m$ do not enter. A larger cache, a
faster hit path, or pinning that delays the onset of losses can only change $s$ on a
finite set of queue lengths. By part~(ii), none of them moves the stability limit
$\lambda<1/S^{\mathrm{miss}}$. What moves it is anything that lowers the tail work: a
faster prefill, or a cheaper way to restore a lost prefix, such as reloading it from
host memory instead of recomputing it. This is the dynamic counterpart of
\cref{L6:prop:collapse}(i), where $\lambda\E[S^{\mathrm{miss}}]\ge1$ made all-miss an
equilibrium.

Mathematical stability is not the whole story. Between $1/S^{\mathrm{miss}}$ and
$1/S^{\mathrm{hit}}$ lies a band of loads at which the queue has no stationary law, yet
the empty queue is a stable fluid equilibrium. There the good state is metastable: it
lasts for the time needed to climb past the fluid saddle, which is exponential in the
barrier. \Cref{L7:tab:open} shows this for $S^{\mathrm{hit}}=0.5$\,s,
$S^{\mathrm{miss}}=5$\,s and a logistic $m$ centred at 20 queued turns (width 4). Just
above the limit the collapse takes $10^{16}$\,s. At ten times the limit it takes
seconds. A load test of finite length sees a stable system in the first case and an
unstable one in the second, although both are unstable. Capacity planning needs the
barrier, not only the limit.

\section{The closed population: hysteresis and exponential recovery}\label{L7:sec:closed}

A closed population of $N$ sessions is a finite chain, so it is always stable. Its
failure mode is that it stays in the bad mode for a long time. Take the same $s$ with
$m$ logistic, centred at $N/4$ with width $N/20$, and $Z=1.5N$\,s, so that every
per-session quantity is fixed while $N$ grows. \Cref{L7:tab:scaling} gives the exact
recovery time from the bad mode to the good one and the inverse spectral gap of the
generator. The barrier grows linearly in $N$, by about $0.21$ per session, and the
recovery time grows like $e^{\Delta V}$, as \cref{L7:prop:arrhenius} predicts. The inverse
gap grows exponentially too. A cluster of eigenvalues near zero, the signature
of~\cite{alvaro2025}, appears here as one small gap, because the chain has two wells.

\paragraph{Hysteresis.} Fix $N=40$ and vary the think time. Two modes exist for
$25\le Z\le120$\,s. Starting from the
good mode, the replica collapses in fluid terms only when the turn rate rises above about
$N/25$ per second. Starting from the bad mode, it recovers only when the rate falls below
about $N/120$. The load that causes the collapse and the load needed to recover differ by
a factor of almost five. Between them, which mode the replica occupies depends on its
history. This is the first question of the issue, answered for this model.

\section{Steady state and recovery rank differently}\label{L7:sec:interventions}

An intervention changes $s$, and \cref{L7:cor:local} says where a change matters for
recovery: only above the target level. The stationary mean, by contrast, depends on
every level. The two criteria can therefore disagree.

\begin{proposition}[Steady state and recovery rank differently]\label{L7:prop:flip}
On $\{0,1,2\}$ with $\lambda_0=\lambda_1=1$ and $\mu_1=\mu_2=1$, intervention A sets
$\mu_1=4$ and intervention B sets $\mu_2=2$. Then A has the smaller stationary mean
queue, $\tfrac12$ against $\tfrac45$, while B has the shorter recovery from 2 to 1,
$\tfrac12$ against 1.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Under A the weights are $(1,\tfrac14,\tfrac14)$, with mean
$(\tfrac14+\tfrac12)/\tfrac32=\tfrac12$. Under B they are $(1,1,\tfrac12)$, with mean
$(1+1)/\tfrac52=\tfrac45$. The recovery from 2 to 1 is $T_2=1/\mu_2$, which is 1 under A
and $\tfrac12$ under B.
\end{proof}

The same thing happens at serving scale. In the closed chain of \cref{L7:sec:closed}
with $N=40$ and $Z=60$\,s, the mean queue is \metaFlipBase{} turns. Halving the service
time at queue lengths up to 6, a faster hit path that only the lightly loaded states
use, lowers it to \metaFlipLow{}. Shortening it by 10\% above 6 lowers it only to
\metaFlipHigh{}. But only the second shortens the recovery from 30 to 6 turns, from
$\metaFlipRecBase$ to $\metaFlipRecHigh$\,s. The first leaves the recovery unchanged, by
\cref{L7:cor:local}(i).

\section{Is the queue length enough?}\label{L7:sec:enough}

The birth--death chain is a projection. A real replica also has a cache state: which
waiting turns still have their prefix, and which thinking sessions will return as
misses. The next lemma says what the projection keeps.

\begin{lemma}[Lumping preserves the stationary marginal]\label{L7:lem:lump}
Let $X$ be a positive recurrent CTMC with stationary law $\pi$ and let $q(X)\in\N$ change
by at most one per transition. Let $\bar\lambda_n$ and $\bar\mu_n$ be the rates of the
moves $q\to q+1$ and $q\to q-1$, averaged over $\pi$ conditioned on $q=n$. Then the
birth--death chain with rates $\bar\lambda_n,\bar\mu_n$ has the stationary law
$p(n)=\pi(q=n)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Sum the global balance equations over the set $A_n=\{x:q(x)\le n\}$. The flows inside
$A_n$ cancel, so the flow out of $A_n$ equals the flow into it. Since $q$ moves by at most
one, only moves between the levels $n$ and $n+1$ cross the boundary:
$\sum_{q(x)=n}\pi(x)\lambda(x)=\sum_{q(x)=n+1}\pi(x)\mu(x)$, that is,
$p(n)\bar\lambda_n=p(n+1)\bar\mu_{n+1}$. This is detailed balance for the lumped chain,
so $p$ is its stationary law by \cref{L2:lem:detailed}.
\end{proof}

So the queue length is enough for every stationary quantity, if the averaged service
rate is known. It is not enough for dynamics. To see how far it falls short, we use a
chain that keeps the cache.

\begin{definition}[Queue-and-cache chain]\label{L7:def:three}
$N$ sessions. A state $(h,m,c)$ counts the queued turns whose prefix is resident ($h$),
the queued turns whose prefix is lost ($m$), and the thinking sessions whose prefix is
lost ($c$). The remaining $w=N-h-m-c$ sessions think with a resident prefix, and
$q=h+m$. Warm sessions return at rate $w/Z$ as hits and cold ones at rate $c/Z$ as
misses. The queue is served by processor sharing, so hits complete at rate $h/(qS^{\mathrm{hit}})$
and misses at rate $m/(qS^{\mathrm{miss}})$. A completed turn leaves a warm thinker. The
idle prefixes $w+h$ must fit in $C-aq$, since every queued turn takes $a$ units of
memory. After each transition, while they do not fit, one is evicted: a queued hit with
probability $1-\phi$ (LRU, since its last use is the oldest) and a warm thinker with
probability $\phi$.
\end{definition}

The parameter $\phi$ is cache protection: $\phi=1$ pins waiting prefixes and evicts
thinking ones instead. We take $N=40$, $Z=27$\,s, $C=48$, $a=1$ and the work of
\cref{L7:sec:open}. The chain has \metaStates{} states and a bimodal queue-length law,
with probability \metaPBad{} of at least 16 queued turns. Its lumped chain reproduces the
marginal law to $\metaMarginalDiff$, as \cref{L7:lem:lump} requires. The dynamics differ:
\begin{itemize}
\item the slowest decay rate of the full chain is \metaSlowThree{}/s, against a gap of
  \metaGapOne{}/s for the lumped chain;
\item the mean recovery from the bad region to $q\le1$ is \metaRecThree\,s in the full
  chain and \metaRecOne\,s in the lumped one, which underestimates it by a factor of
  about three;
\item two states with the same empty queue behave in opposite ways
  (\cref{L7:tab:warmcold}). From a warm start, the bad region has probability 0.005 after
  30\,s. From a cold start, where every session has lost its prefix, the probability is
  0.96.
\end{itemize}

The lost-prefix count is the hidden slow variable, as the orbit is in
\cite{alvaro2025}. A model that predicts recovery has to carry it, or an equivalent
summary. The averaged queue-length chain is right for steady state and wrong for
recovery, and it errs in the optimistic direction in this model.

\paragraph{Bursts.} Start warm and empty, shorten the think time to 12\,s for $B$
seconds, and restore it (\cref{L7:tab:burst}). Without a burst, the bad region has
probability 0.35 after 600\,s. A 30\,s burst raises this to 0.60, and longer bursts add
little: the burst has filled the bad well, and the time to leave it is set by the
barrier, not by the burst. The mean time back to an empty queue is about 1500\,s, fifty
times the burst.

\paragraph{Interventions.} \Cref{L7:tab:interventions} ranks seven arms, each a single
change to the base chain, by mean response time (Little's law,
$\E q/\text{throughput}$) and by mean recovery time from the bad region. A faster
prefill wins in steady state. A slightly larger cache wins in recovery. Speeding up
only the miss path, the work that by \cref{L7:thm:open} sets the stability limit of an
open queue, helps least here. In this closed population the onset of losses is set
by the queue length at which $w+h$ overflows, and the hit work moves that onset.
Protection helps and is monotone in $\phi$, because the chain charges nothing for
pinned memory. Whether partial protection beats full protection, the second question of
the issue, depends on a concurrency cost of pinning that this chain does not contain.

\section{What is exact and what is approximate}\label{L7:sec:honest}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Exact, given the model:} \cref{L7:prop:modes,L7:prop:passage,L7:cor:local,%
L7:prop:arrhenius,L7:thm:open,L7:prop:flip,L7:lem:lump}, and every number in the tables,
  which are computed from the generators rather than simulated. The statements of
  \cref{L7:sec:modes,L7:sec:passage,L7:sec:open,L7:sec:interventions} are also checked in
  Lean (module \texttt{Metastability}); \cref{L7:lem:lump} is not.
\item \emph{Modelling choices:} exponential service with a state-dependent mean;
  processor sharing in place of the FIFO prefill queue in the queue-and-cache chain; a
  loss probability $m(n)$, or an overflow rule, in place of block-level LRU; memory
  counted in prefixes of equal size; no cost of pinned memory.
\item \emph{To be measured:} the shape of $m$, the slack $C-N$ and the per-turn memory
  $a$ on a real engine. The burst protocol of the issue (normal load, a short burst,
  normal load; warm and cold starts) is the test. A finite run cannot establish
  instability, and the open-queue table shows why: a lifetime of $10^{16}$\,s and a
  stable queue look the same.
\end{itemize}

\section{Exercises}

\begin{exercise}\label{L7:ex:mm1}
For the open queue with $s(n)\equiv S$, recover the M/M/1 stability condition from
\cref{L7:thm:open}, and compute $T_k$ from \cref{L7:prop:passage} as $M\to\infty$.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}\label{L7:ex:step}
Let $m(n)=\1\{n>K\}$ in the open queue, with $1/S^{\mathrm{miss}}<\lambda<1/S^{\mathrm{hit}}$.
Show that the fluid saddle is at $K$, and that the mean time to exceed $K$ from the
empty queue is of order $(\lambda S^{\mathrm{hit}})^{-K}$.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}\label{L7:ex:pin}
Add a concurrency cost to \cref{L7:def:three}: pinned prefixes slow every turn by the
factor $1+\gamma\,\phi h/N$. Find, numerically, a $\gamma$ at which an interior $\phi$
minimises the recovery time.
\end{exercise}
