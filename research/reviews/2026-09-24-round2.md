## Verdict

**Major revision.** v0.8 is a different paper from v0.7: the thesis is
now stated at the level the evidence supports, the textbook facts are
cited rather than claimed, the closed-system reading of the replay is
correct, and the trace work (variance split, p and tau by phase) is the
kind of workload evidence the field lacks. I would now expect ICML
reviewers to argue about the contribution rather than dismiss it. I do
not yet recommend acceptance because the paper's central quantity still
has a gap between what is proved (an open M/G/1 price) and where it is
used (a replica with 2 to 16 live sessions), and the paper's own
treatment of that gap is internally inconsistent: the abstract calls
the open price "an upper bound when few sessions are live", the body
says it is "conservative" and "loose by several times", and the
simulator report behind those words shows one cell where the observed
rise is three quarters of the price (not "a quarter to a seventh") and
two cells where it exceeds the linear price by up to 3.4 times. Those
numbers are typed by hand, appear in no table, and the generated
finite-source table that would settle the in-model question is not in
the paper.

**Single biggest remaining weakness:** the finite-source gap is now
described but not resolved, and the description does not match the
data. Fixing the description is a day; adding the M/M/1//N proposition
(action item 3) is what turns the weakness into a result. Second: 0.7
page over the limit with an experimental design in the main text.

### 3. §2.3 and Table 2: one false sentence and an unmotivated hit rate

Where: "Forcing 4 % of turns to miss adds the mixture, which then
supplies 57 % of the variance under a linear cost model and a third of
it when attention dominates. The append is thus at least half of the
variance at the reuse these traces show".

What is wrong: under the linear model the mixture share is 58 % at
p = 0.90, 57 % at 0.96 and 47 % at 0.99 (Table 2), so the append is
"at least half" only at p = 0.99 or under the attention models. The two
sentences contradict each other as written. Also the choice p = 0.96
is not motivated; the observed reuse is 98.8 % and the replay's tight
cap gives 0.80 to 0.86.

Fix: "Under the linear model the mixture supplies 47 to 58 % of
Var[S] for hit rates between 0.99 and 0.90, and 23 to 35 % once
attention dominates; the append is never less than 42 % of the variance
at these hit rates, and the mixture becomes the larger source only once
eviction is active (hit rate 0.80 to 0.86 in the replay of §4.2, where
it supplies 41 to 75 %)." Otherwise the section is convincing and
properly hedged: "the prefill-work split rests on an assumed cost model"
is in Limitations, Table 2 states K_c, and the abstract's "prefill
variance comes from the appends as much as from misses" is a fair
one-line summary once the sentence above is fixed. The caption's column
name "Observed reuse" does not match the header "all hit"; use one.

### 5. Algorithm 1 step 3 vs `threshold_rule_optimal_byte_seconds`

Where: §3.1 "The same holds per byte-second, with c_i tau_i in place of
c_i, which is the form Algorithm 1 uses"; Algorithm 1 step 3 "Set theta
to the smallest value at which the blocks with u_i <= theta free
Delta C".

What is wrong: the Lean statement is optimality among sets that free at
least as many byte-seconds (`sum c_i tau_i` over the threshold set <=
`sum c_i tau_i` over S). Step 3 stops when the threshold set frees
Delta C bytes. A set of short-tau states can free Delta C bytes with few
byte-seconds; the theorem says nothing about it. The mismatch of M4(b)
has moved from the proposition to the algorithm.

Fix: either state step 3 as "free Delta C bytes for the horizon H, i.e.
Delta C . H byte-seconds" and define H (the mean remaining suspension of
the evicted set, or the next decision epoch), or write in §3.1: "the
algorithm frees a byte target with the byte-second order, a heuristic
mapping that the simulation tests (Table 10)". One sentence either way.

### 8. The 600 s split rule shapes the replay more than the paper admits

Where: Table 5 caption "a gap above 10 min starts a new session"; §4.2
"(median 253k tokens against 0k for a genuinely new session; 77 % of
replayed sessions start this way)".

What is wrong: 77 % of the 761 replayed sessions are continuations cut
by the split rule, each opening with a cold prefill of about 253k
tokens. The open-pool TTFT of 5 to 11 s and the CV^2 of 35 to 43 are
therefore largely a property of the rule. The rule is defensible (a
production prefix cache with a minutes-scale TTL would miss after ten
minutes) but the paper does not defend it or vary it. "0k for a
genuinely new session" says that a new session's first context is
empty, which is a definition, not a statistic.

Fix: one sentence of justification (cache TTL), and one sensitivity
row or macro: the open-pool TTFT with splits at 30 min and with no
split (KV kept across gaps). Replace "against 0k for a genuinely new
session" with "a genuinely new session starts from an empty context".
Add the mean live count and the throughput X to Table 5 so the reader
can check "five sessions live" and the closed-system throughput.

### 9. Prop. 3(ii) statement vs its proof

"If every program resumes and its price is its recompute work alone, so
that w_i = c_i^2 by (1)": by (1) the recompute work is a c_i + b c_i^2/2,
and the proof says "the proposition takes the quadratic term as the
price". Write "so that, in the attention-dominated regime of (1),
w_i is proportional to c_i^2" or state w_i = c_i^2 as the hypothesis
without "by (1)". Same in §3.3: "shortest-first sets w_i = c_i^2".

### 11. The "two facts" paragraph (§2.4): honest, two clauses missing

The monotonicity sentence is used honestly (it is stated as
"nondecreasing in the context K_i" at "one load" and the conclusion
"the load alone never reorders programs by their price" follows). It
needs "at equal append n_i" (the Lean statement fixes `n`). The flip
example is used honestly: two loads, two orders, "which no key fixed in
advance reproduces" is a logical consequence. It should say the order
is per byte (tau equal) and name the cost model (a = 2e-5, b = 4e-10,
the replay's). The mechanism should be named in one clause: the flip
comes from the second term, (S^miss + S^hit)/2 times larger for the
long program, which is the head-of-line term the paper wants the reader
to remember.
