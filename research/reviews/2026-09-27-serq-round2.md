## Remaining

R2-1. **The two overhead constants are effective, not physical.** The
served step trace gives median step periods of 18.7 ms (decode) and 42 ms
(a 512-token prefill chunk) against 14 ms and 41–50 ms stepped by hand:
the served path is slower per decode step and no faster per prefill
chunk, so the fitted `c_it = 4 ms` is not "what asynchronous scheduling
hides". A regression of served periods on serQ's step quantities is too
noisy to replace the fit (MAPE 25–56 %). The pair (4 ms, 40 ms) should be
presented as a light-load calibration that predicts the loaded runs, with
the ridge stated, and the per-request constant attributed to the text
front end only as a hypothesis.

R2-2. **Lecture 1's Algorithm 1 had no iteration events**; fixed in this
round (the earliest iteration end enters Δ).

R2-3. **The pre-registered predictions** (`data/exp/seq/prereg/
predictions.txt`, recorded before the traced runs finished) are the test
of the calibrated model: 3.0 s vLLM predicted 0.605 s / 0.775, measured
0.441 s / 0.832. The H-pin runs decide whether the one-line change of the
admission rule moves the cliff on the real engine.
