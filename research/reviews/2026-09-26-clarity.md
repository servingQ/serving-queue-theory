# Clarity and flow review, 2026-09-26

Scope: `paper/main.tex`, `paper/simulation.tex`, `paper/simulation-appendix.tex`
as of the working tree on 2026-09-26. Reviewer stance: an ICML ML-systems
reviewer reading the draft for the first time. This is a clarity and flow
review only; nothing below is a verdict on correctness or on the numbers.
Line numbers refer to `main.tex` unless marked `sim.tex`.

## 1. Passages a first-time reader cannot follow

Ordered by where they occur. Each item quotes the opening words, says what
blocks the reader, and gives a rewrite that adds no claim and types no number.

### 1.1 Abstract, l.88: "An agentic LLM server keeps the KV cache of suspended programs across tool calls, and every KV decision asks what losing one costs."

"One" has no clear referent (a program? a cache?). Later, "the open wait
bounds the Markovian wait" and "decode prices only work" are unreadable
without the paper, and "the cap on live sessions decides" does not say what
it decides. Suggested abstract (about 150 words; trim as needed):

> An agentic LLM server keeps a program's KV cache across tool calls, and
> every KV decision asks what one miss costs. Sessions loop through turns
> and tools on replicas with a prefill queue, a decode batch and one memory
> pool; we derive the price of a miss, the time to first token it adds
> summed over all turns. Beyond its recompute, a miss delays every prefill
> queued behind it, a quadratic term that chunked prefill does not remove;
> the decode batch sees only mean work and is unchanged. One shadow price of
> memory decides eviction, offloading, placement and admission. With few
> sessions live the prefill queue is closed, and the open-queue wait is an
> upper bound. On an NPU testbed replaying coding-agent sessions, a miss
> waits \eTwoMissOverHitLo{} to \eTwoMissOverHitHi{} times longer for its
> first token than a hit, mostly for KV blocks; the cap on live sessions
> sets that wait.

### 1.2 §1, l.112: "The serving question is therefore not how many tokens per second..."

TTFT and SLA are used here, 80 lines before the Background paragraph
defines TTFT; SLA is never defined. Rewrite: "The serving question is
therefore not how many tokens per second a device delivers but which
control knob moves the load at which the time to first token (TTFT)
crosses its service-level target."

### 1.3 §1, l.117: "the median session has \wkTurnsMedian{} LLM turns over a context of..."

"Turn", "append" and "resident prefix" are undefined until §2. Rewrite the
second clause: "and almost every turn (one LLM request of the session)
adds a tool result to a prompt whose earlier tokens the server has already
processed and may still hold in memory (§2)."

### 1.4 §1, contribution 1, l.150: "we derive its price at the prefill queue as the marginal externality of an M/G/1 job whose size grows"

"Marginal externality", "M/G/1", "finite-source system", "Markovian form"
and "open-queue wait" arrive in three lines with no gloss. Rewrite: "A miss
changes only the prefill work. We derive its price at the prefill queue as
the delay one longer job adds to itself and to the jobs behind it
(Proposition 1); the decode batch depends on work only through its mean,
so a miss leaves it unchanged (Proposition 3). With N sessions live a
waiting session cannot submit its next turn; for exponential work we prove
that the usual open-queue wait then overstates the true wait
(Proposition 2)."

### 1.5 §1, contribution 2, l.157: "One price per suspended program, its expected price per byte-second of memory, is the variable shared by..."

One 75-word sentence introduces byte-second, shadow price, head-of-line
term, insensitivity, covering knapsack, threshold rule, guarded greedy and
"key" (as a sort key). Rewrite: "The scheduler compares one number per
suspended program, its expected price per byte of memory per second held,
with one shadow price of memory. That comparison drives eviction,
offloading, placement and admission. Eviction is a covering knapsack whose
standard solutions we cite. Any eviction rule that ignores resume
probabilities can be made arbitrarily bad, and shortest-first is the
special case in which the price is the recompute alone (Proposition 4)."

### 1.6 §2 Background, l.195, versus Table 1, l.233: "its duration is the time to first token (TTFT)" versus "TTFT = W_q + S"

The Background defines TTFT as the prefill's duration (service only); Table
1 and the whole paper use wait plus service. Rewrite the Background
sentence: "Prefill reads the whole prompt in one pass and produces the
first token; the time to first token (TTFT) is the wait for the prefill to
start plus its duration."

### 1.7 §2.1, l.262: "For networks of processor-sharing and delay stations the BCMP theorem gives a product-form stationary law"

"Delay station", "product form" and "the same reading" are not explained.
Rewrite: "For a network of processor-sharing stations and delay stations
(stations without queueing, such as the tool call) the BCMP theorem
\citep{bcmp1975} gives a product-form stationary law: each station behaves
as if fed by Poisson arrivals at its own rate, whatever the tool-time law.
The decode stage is such a station. The FIFO prefill stage is not, and we
use the same Poisson reading for it as an approximation, tested in §4.2."

### 1.8 §2.2, l.273 and l.286: "Prefilling n tokens onto a resident prefix of K" then "with n the batch size"

In adjacent bullets `n` is a token count in (1) and the batch size in (2),
and §2.2 then says φ(n) is "in units of (2)", which does not parse. Use a
different letter for the batch size (say `m`) in (2), in φ(m) and in
Proposition 3, and write "so that a batch of m turns completes φ(m)
seconds of demand (2) per second".

### 1.9 §2.2, l.313: "We therefore model the prefill stage as a FIFO queue with service P_i whose server is available a fraction 1−ρ_D of the time."

The next sentence uses ρ in (2) without saying what it is, and §2.3 uses
ρ_P without ever defining it. The relation between ρ, ρ_D and λE[S] is
never written. Add one sentence after (2) that defines the prefill load
(the authors know the intended relation; the draft does not state it),
then say "we write ρ for the prefill load from here on".

### 1.10 §2.4, l.406: "In the stationary model a policy fixes which fraction of arrivals are misses, so we ask what happens to L_P when a fraction q_i of arrivals, belonging to program i, turn from hits into misses."

The reader expects the cost of evicting one program and instead gets a
stationary fraction q_i; the sentence that explains why ("Applied to one
eviction ... Φ_i is an approximation") comes 40 lines later at l.449.
Move it here: "A single eviction is a transient event. In the stationary
model we price it as a small change of policy: a fraction q_i of arrivals,
those of program i, turn from hits into misses, and we ask how L_P
responds. Applied to one eviction this is an approximation, and §4.2
measures how loose."

### 1.11 After Proposition 1, l.429 and l.446: "Φ_i is the stationary marginal price: the time to first token, summed over all turns, that one more miss of program i adds." and "once the mean wait exceeds half the miss"

The units are left to the reader (Φ_i in seconds, λq_iΦ_i a number of
turns), and "half the miss" is loose. Rewrite: "Φ_i is in seconds: the
TTFT, summed over all turns, that one extra miss of program i adds. Misses
of program i occur at rate λq_i, so λΣ_i q_iΦ_i is a rise in the mean
number in the prefill stage, by Little's law." and "once the mean wait W
exceeds half the sum of the miss and hit work, the load term takes over".

### 1.12 §2.4, l.452: "so the load alone never reorders programs by price; it reorders them by price per byte-second once p_i or τ_i differ."

The next sentence says two programs "change order between an idle queue
and ρ=0.5", which reads as a contradiction of "never reorders". State which
quantity flips. Rewrite: "so the load never reorders programs of equal
append by Φ_i alone. It does reorder them by the expected price p_iΦ_i
once p_i differs: under the cost model of Appendix B, [example], the two
change order in p_iΦ_i between an idle queue and ρ=0.5, which no fixed key
reproduces." (Use whichever of Φ_i, p_iΦ_i or u_i the example actually
compares.)

### 1.13 §2.4, l.465: "The M/G/1 model has one more assumption that a replica with long contexts violates: an unbounded population. ... The Markovian finite-source model makes the correction exact."

"The correction" has no referent. Rewrite: "The M/G/1 model assumes an
unbounded population. A pool of a few million tokens holds about ten
sessions of the workload of §4.1, and a session waiting for prefill cannot
submit its next turn, so fewer turns arrive while the queue is long. For
exponential work the finite-source model captures this exactly."

### 1.14 Algorithm 1, l.553 and l.561: "Set θ to the smallest value at which the blocks with u_i ≤ θ free ΔC" and "Place a resuming turn at arg min_j W_j+S_j+M_j+F_j"

u_i is a per-program quantity but step 3 speaks of blocks; step 6 uses
F_j, M_j and "node j" before §3.2 defines any of them. Rewrite step 3:
"Set θ to the smallest value at which the states (or their blocks) with
u_i ≤ θ free ΔC". Rewrite step 6: "Place a resuming turn at the replica j
that minimises W_j+S_j+M_j+F_j (wait, service, move cost and future cost at
j, §3.2), with M_j = Φ_i at replica j's load, or the priced fetch if the
state is in a shared store."

### 1.15 §3.3, l.683 to l.702: "The memory pool couples the two roles of KV, and the coupling is unstable"

The two roles are never named; `D` in Campbell's formula clashes with the
decode demand D_i; M/G/∞ is undefined; and "the shadow price of the cap is
the entry wait" introduces a second shadow price beside θ without saying
they are different objects. Rewrite: "The pool holds both the batch's
working set and the suspended states, and the two compete: a miss holds
its turn in the prefill queue and then in the batch for longer, ... Let a
session live for time T_s and hold K(t) bytes at age t. Treating live
sessions as an M/G/∞ system (each session is its own server, none
queues), Campbell's theorem gives the mean resident KV as
Λ E[∫_0^{T_s} K(t) dt], ... The cap has a price of its own, the wait at the
entry queue: a session is worth admitting while the memory it will hold,
at θ per byte-second, costs less than that wait."

## 3. Flow

**Abstract.** Not understandable on its own: three of its six sentences
need §2 (see 1.1). It also does not say what the testbed result means for
the thesis.

**§1.** The first paragraph is queueing 101 and reads well. The thesis
("the price of a miss") appears only in the third paragraph; the
contributions are dense (contribution 2 alone carries eight new terms).
The intro would flow better if each contribution stated the claim in two
sentences and left the mechanism to §2 and §3.

**§2 → §3.** The handoff is good: §2.4 ends with (6) and §3 opens by
picking up u_i and θ. Two things break the flow inside §2.4. First,
Proposition 2 (finite population) sits between Proposition 1 (price) and
Proposition 3 (decode), so the two-stage story ("the prefill queue pays,
the decode batch does not") is interrupted by the population correction.
Consider price → decode → finite. Second, the caveat about pricing a
transient eviction as a stationary fraction comes after the proposition
(1.10).

**A wait the model has no name for.** §2.2 says a replica "admits turns
into the batch until the next one does not fit", and that is the only
place a turn can wait for memory. §4.3's headline finding is that the
observed wait is for KV blocks, not prefill compute, and the abstract
leads with it. A first-time reader cannot locate that wait in Table 1 or
in T = W_q + S. One sentence in the memory-pool paragraph of §2.2 ("a turn
that does not fit waits for blocks before its prefill starts; that wait is
part of W_q") and one in §4.3 tying the measured queueing time to it would
close the gap.

**§3.** Algorithm 1 up front, then the steps derived, works. The
subsection order (eviction, placement, admission and offloading) does not
follow the step order (offload is step 5, place is step 6); harmless but
worth a clause. The last sentence of §3.3 ("Every rule in use replaces one
price by a constant") is the summary of the whole section and would sit
better as §3's closing paragraph, outside the offloading discussion.

**§4.** Traces → simulation → testbed is easy to follow once the opening
sentence stops calling the testbed the most controlled source (see the
smaller items). The simulation subsection opens with a one-sentence
summary of the seeded checks that belongs in Appendix B; the main text
needs only "the closed forms are reproduced within 2% (Appendix B)". The
replay paragraph and the price paragraph each need splitting.

**§5, §6.** Fine in position. §5's sentence "We claim the application ...,
not the formula" is the clearest statement of novelty in the paper and
could be echoed in the intro. §6's "what would falsify" list is good.

**Sentences that belong elsewhere.**
- §2.3 l.384: "The mixture rivals or exceeds the appends only once eviction
  is active (... in the replay of §4.2)" is a simulation result inside the
  model section; move to §4.2.
- §2.4 l.449: the "applied to one eviction" caveat, move before
  Proposition 1 (1.10).
- §2.4 l.458: "Under the cost model of Appendix B (K_c=50k tokens)" points
  the reader to an appendix for the parameters of a main-text example;
  state the two parameters inline.
- §3.3 l.714: the last sentence, move to close §3.
- sim.tex l.24 to l.33: the seeded-check summary, move to Appendix B.
