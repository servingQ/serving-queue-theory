# Review, round 1 (2026-09-24), draft v0.7

Reviewer role: queueing theory / systems, ICML area chair. Read in order:
AGENTS.md; paper/main.tex, paper/simulation.tex and every generated
table under paper/sim/ and paper/traces/ that the text refers to;
research/research-plan.md, research/trace-analysis.md, research/simulation-design.md;
the Lean statements for every `\provedby` in the propositions
(MissPrice, BatchServer, DensityGreedy, Eviction, Routing); the rendered
PDF (16 pages; main text through Limitations ends on page 12, references
start on page 13). Data files paper/sim/data/{trace,inversion}.csv were
consulted for the columns the tables omit (measured rho, live sessions,
entry wait).

## Summary of the paper as I understood it

The paper models an agentic LLM replica as a FIFO prefill queue that
receives the compute a processor-sharing decode batch leaves, plus one
memory pool shared by the batch and the KV of suspended programs, fed by
Poisson sessions that loop through turns and tool calls. Its central
object is the "price of a miss" Phi_i (Prop. 1): the first-order change in
the mean number of turns at an M/G/1 prefill queue when a fraction of a
program's turns become misses, decomposed into recompute, a head-of-line
term quadratic in the miss, and a load term; the decode stage is shown to
be insensitive to misses (Prop. 2). Section 3 builds a scheduler on the
per-byte-second price u_i = p_i Phi_i /(c_i tau_i) compared with a memory
shadow price theta, with covering-knapsack facts for eviction (Props. 3
to 5), an M/M/1 inversion load for session affinity (Prop. 6), and a
keep/offload/drop rule; published keys (shortest-first, LRU, TTL,
always-offload, strict affinity) are read as the special cases in which
one price is a constant. Section 4.1 is an uncalibrated simulation that
reproduces the closed forms in-model, then drops assumptions (batch cap,
emergent hit rate, four replicas with a shared link, replayed production
Claude Code sessions) and finds that admission and thrashing dominate,
that eviction order is within seed noise, and that the open M/G/1 wait
overstates the observed wait 4 to 20 times for 2 to 24 live sessions.
Section 4.2 is an experimental design (E1 to E6) with no measurements,
plus trace statistics from a public Claude Code proxy corpus.

## Verdict

**Not acceptable at ICML as is.** Three reasons, in order of severity.

1. The evidence does not support the method. The paper's own simulation
   says the congestion terms of Phi_i do not change eviction decisions
   beyond seed noise (Table 6: "no cell separates it from shortest-first
   or from plain price-per-byte order beyond the seed noise"; Table 7 at
   cap 32: thrashing regardless of rule), that admission decides the
   regime, and that the M/G/1 model of the prefill queue overstates the
   wait 4 to 20 times on replayed sessions. Algorithm 1's admission step,
   the one step the simulation says matters, is the least specified (one
   sentence, no proposition, never simulated as stated; the sweep uses a
   fixed cap). The abstract nevertheless says "no fixed rule ... ranks
   states correctly at every load", a claim no proposition proves and the
   simulation does not show.
2. Novelty. Props. 3 to 5 are the fractional-knapsack threshold rule, the
   Dantzig greedy plus one item, the min-knapsack greedy-with-guess
   2-approximation (Csirik et al. 1991, which the paper itself cites for
   approximability), and the observation that a rule that does not see
   p_i can be fooled by p_i. Prop. 1 is the classical M/G/1 marginal
   externality (Mendelson and Whang 1990 have the size-squared term in a
   priority-pricing setting), applied to KV misses. Prop. 6 is that an
   M/M/1 wait is unbounded. The contribution is the framing and the
   unification, which is legitimate, but the paper presents six
   propositions as results, and an ICML reviewer will read them as
   relabelled textbook facts.
3. Form. 12.3 pages of main text against an 8-page limit; a
   1.5-page experimental design with `TBD` in the main text; 8 simulation
   tables plus 3 figures in 4.5 pages for a section the paper itself calls
   uncalibrated and illustrative.

**Single biggest weakness:** the paper's central quantity, an open M/G/1
price, is derived for a regime (many independent arrivals, load below
one) that the paper's own memory model forbids: a 4M-token pool with
mean contexts of 391k tokens holds about ten live sessions, so the
prefill queue of a long-context replica is a small finite-source system,
and Table 9 shows the open price is off by an order of magnitude there.
Either the price is restated for the closed/finite-source model or its
role is narrowed to ranking, and the paper must say which.

What is good, and worth keeping: the two-resource formulation (Table 1)
is clean and correct about where hit/miss lives (prefill, not decode);
the insensitivity argument for decode is right and well used; the
"append vs mixture" decomposition of prefill variance with a trace
baseline is exactly the honest stance the field needs; the refutation
of ThunderAgent's shortest-first optimality theorem is correct and
useful; the simulation is unusually honest about its own model failing
(LPS error, PK overstatement, thrashing); Lean-checked statements match
the prose closely (I checked every `\provedby` in Props. 1 to 6); the
writing is mostly clear.

## Major issues

### M1. The evidence contradicts the pitch (abstract, §1, §3 vs §4.1)

Where: abstract ("no fixed rule, shortest-context-first included, ranks
states correctly at every load"), §1 contribution 2, §3 opening, Table 6
and Table 7 text.

What is wrong: (a) Nothing in the paper proves the abstract's ranking
claim. Prop. 5(i) is about ignoring p_i, not about load. The only
support is the prose after Prop. 1: "A rule fixed in advance ... ranks
states the way Phi does only in some load regime." Under AGENTS.md rule 1
this is an unproved claim in the abstract. (b) It is also not obviously
true within a replica: for programs on the same replica, every term of
Phi_i is increasing in K_i under Eq. (1), so the order by Phi_i alone is
the order by context length at every load; the load enters the eviction
order only through its interaction with p_i and tau_i (the ratio
Phi_i/c_i moves from ~ Delta S_i/c_i at low load to ~ Delta S_i (S_i^miss
+ S_i^hit)/c_i at high load, so a long, low-p program and a short, high-p
program can swap). (c) The simulation shows the congestion terms do not
separate policies end-to-end (Table 6, Table 7). The paper reports this
honestly in §4.1 but the abstract and §1 do not reflect it.

Fix: (1) prove what the abstract says: a two-program Lean example
`(K_1,p_1), (K_2,p_2)` whose u_i order flips between two loads, cited
inline; and state in §2.5 that within a replica Phi_i is monotone in K_i
so the load acts through p_i and tau_i and through cross-decision
comparisons (offload, admission, routing), not the eviction order by
itself. (2) Rewrite abstract and §1 to what the paper shows: the price
unifies eviction, offloading, admission and placement under one shadow
price; the congestion terms matter for the scale (which decides offload
vs recompute and admission) and for TTFT, while the eviction order is
mostly decided by p_i, tau_i and size; admission decides throughput.
(3) Either specify the admission step as a rule (a proposition on the
theta-admission rule in the M/G/inf resident-KV model would be the
natural one) and simulate it as stated, or downgrade Algorithm 1 step 7
to "an admission cap set by the resident-KV estimate of §2.1", which is
what the simulation actually runs.

### M2. The open M/G/1 prefill queue is the wrong regime for long contexts (§2.2, Prop. 1, Table 9)

Where: §2.2 "Prefill stage", Prop. 1, §4.1 "Replayed production
sessions", Limitations.

What is wrong: Table 9 (paper/sim/data/trace.csv) shows the tight-cap
cells run with 2, 4, 8 or 16 live sessions, measured rho between 0.55
and 0.98, and entry waits of 5 to 13 hours (19 678 to 46 129 s). Two
things follow that the text does not say. First, those cells are not an
open system at rate Lambda at all: the cap binds, the entry queue grows
without bound over the horizon, and the replica is a closed network with
N = cap. The 1M/cap-2 rows at Lambda = 0.005 and 0.010 are identical to
three digits (hit 0.86, W_q 10, PK 81, TTFT 24), which confirms it. The
"Lambda" column is irrelevant for those rows and the table's caption
("arriving Poisson at rate Lambda") is misleading. Second, the "PK
overstates 4 to 20 times" ratio is computed in cells with rho = 0.976 to
0.984, where the open formula is near its pole and any finite system
will be far below it. The comparison is right in direction and the
explanation (finite source, self-limiting arrivals) is correct, but the
factor is a property of the pole, not a calibrated error. More
important, the same arithmetic applies to production: M/(beta K) for a
4M pool and 391k contexts is about ten sessions, so the "small live
population" is not an artefact of the experiment; it is the regime the
memory model implies whenever contexts are long. Prop. 1's bracket is
then an upper bound of unknown looseness exactly where the paper wants
to use it.

Fix (pick one and say so in §2.2, Prop. 1 and Limitations):
(a) Restate the price for the finite-source model. With N live sessions
and think time Z, the machine-repair / M/G/1//N model gives the mean
wait; a first-order price of a miss in that model is a derivative of
the closed-network response time R = N/X - Z with respect to the demand
of one class. Even the M/M/1//N case in closed form, with the open price
as its N -> inf limit, would make the paper honest and add a real
result (the research plan already lists this as a candidate).
(b) Or keep the open price and reframe it explicitly as an upper bound
that is used for ranking, then show (in Lean or simulation) that the
ranking it induces is preserved under the finite-source correction.
(c) At minimum: add the live-session count and measured rho as columns
of Table 9, state that the tight-cap cells are a closed system with
N = cap, and drop the Lambda dimension for them.

### M3. Propositions 3 to 5 and 6 are known facts presented as results (§3)

Where: Prop. 3 (threshold rule; density prefix plus one), Prop. 4
(density-first unbounded; guarded greedy 2-approx), Prop. 5(ii)
(SF as w = c^2 density order, 2-approx, tight), Prop. 6.

What is wrong: Prop. 3(i) is the Lagrangian/exchange argument for the
fractional knapsack; 3(ii) is the Dantzig greedy plus one item; 4(ii) is
the standard 2-approximation for min-knapsack (covering knapsack) by
guessing the most expensive item and running the density greedy on
items no more expensive than it, which is in Csirik, Frenk, Labbé and
Zhang (1991), the paper's own citation; 4(i) is the standard bad
instance for the unguarded greedy; 5(ii) is a corollary of 4(ii) for a
specific weight function, and 5(i) is a one-line adversary. Prop. 6
says an M/M/1 wait is unbounded and solves 1/(mu - lambda) = 1/mu + M +
F for lambda. None of this is wrong, and the Lean statements match the
prose. But presented as six numbered propositions they invite the
verdict "relabelling". The same applies to Prop. 1(ii) (an algebraic
identity) and 1(iii) (the PK denominator diverges), which pad the
central result.

Fix: Keep every Lean theorem; change the paper's presentation. (1) State
Props. 3, 4(ii) and 5(ii) in prose as known results with citations
(Dantzig 1957; Csirik et al. 1991; Carnes and Shmoys 2008), keeping the
inline `\provedby` since the repo verified them. That is what
AGENTS.md's own convention ("standard results ... in prose with a
citation") requires. (2) Merge what is genuinely the paper's into two
propositions: the price of a miss (Prop. 1(i) only; move (ii) and (iii)
to prose sentences with inline `\provedby`), and one "fixed keys"
proposition combining 5(i), the SF non-optimality instance and the
load-flip example of M1. (3) Demote Prop. 6 to a prose sentence with the
closed form rho^* = x/(1+x) and its inline `\provedby`; its value is the
number, not the theorem. (4) Say in one sentence what is new: the price
Phi_i as the decision variable shared by four decisions, the head-of-line
term that chunking does not remove, the separation of stages by
insensitivity, and the trace-based split of variance. That list is
defensible; six propositions are not.

### M4. Prop. 1's object and the paper's use of it disagree in three places (§2.4, §2.5, Table 1, Algorithm 1)

Where: Table 1 "Objective", Eq. (7), Prop. 1, §2.5 text, Algorithm 1
step 3, Table 6 caption.

What is wrong: (a) L_P is defined as "the mean number of turns waiting
for prefill" (Table 1, Eq. 7: "waiting for prefill plus in the decode
batch"), but the proof and the Lean `numInSystem = lam * pkWait + rho`
count turns in service as well, and the first term Delta S_i of Phi_i is
that in-service part. Table 1 says "Its wait is the TTFT"; the price
counts wait plus prefill service. Define TTFT = W_q + S once and make
L_P the number in the prefill stage (queue plus server). (b) Prop. 3 is
stated for w_i/c_i (price per byte) with a constraint on bytes freed,
but Algorithm 1 step 3 thresholds u_i = w_i/(c_i tau_i) (price per
byte-second) and Table 6 calls "Priced/tau" "the threshold rule of
Prop. 3". Dividing by tau_i changes the objective: the theta-threshold on
u_i is optimal for a constraint on byte-seconds freed, not bytes. Either
state the byte-second variant (constraint sum c_i tau_i >= Delta C tau
for the horizon, with a proof, trivial in Lean by substituting c_i tau_i
for c_i) or say that u_i is a heuristic extension of Prop. 3 that the
simulation tests. (c) Prop. 1 turns "a fraction q_i of arrivals" into
misses and prices the stationary change; the text and Algorithm 1 apply
Phi_i to one eviction event ("the TTFT one miss of program i adds").
Limitations concedes this (transient vs stationary) but §2.5 asserts the
per-event reading as if it were the proposition. Say in §2.5 that Phi_i
is the stationary marginal price and that its per-event use is an
approximation.

Smaller correctness points, same section: Prop. 2 needs a stability
condition (rho_D below the limit of phi) for the untruncated sum in the
appendix proof; Prop. 2(ii) mixes "misses" with "added demand" (a miss
adds no decode demand, so the sum in (ii) is over admitted demand, not
misses; rename q_i Delta D_i there). Prop. 6's alternative is an "idle
node"; the simulation's alternative is the least-loaded of three, and the
paper should say the idle node gives the earliest inversion, so rho^* is
a lower bound on the inversion load of any real alternative.

### M5. §2.3: the toy CV^2 example contradicts the trace baseline it sits next to

Where: §2.3, the sentence "At a 96% hit rate, a hit prefill of 50 ms and
a miss of 5 s give CV^2 > 15, more than 8x the wait of exponential work;
the same hit rate with a 100 ms miss gives CV^2 < 0.05."

What is wrong: two paragraphs earlier the paper says the append alone has
CV^2 = 32 in production traces. The example then takes deterministic
hits (CV^2 of the all-hit law = 0) and shows the mixture raises CV^2 to
15, when the measured all-hit baseline is already 32 (16.5x the
exponential wait). The example therefore understates the baseline and
overstates the mixture's marginal effect; the "CV^2 < 0.05" clause is
false for any workload with a variable append. The trace-derived
decomposition (paper/traces/tab-weka-cv2.tex) is generated but never
`\input`; the paper quotes one cell of it (57%). The mixture's share is
57% under a linear cost model and 35% at K_c = 100K, and it falls with
p; the E2 hypothesis "the prefill wait tracks (1+CV^2)/2" is the wrong
statistic because CV^2 is non-monotone in p (research/trace-analysis.md:
41.3 at p = 0.99, 24.5 at 0.96, 12.3 at 0.90 vs 32.0 all-hit) while E[S^2]
and rho are monotone.

Fix: replace the toy numbers by the trace table (append CV^2 as the
baseline row; the mixture's share at three hit rates and two cost
models), keep the Lean `_example` theorems uncited, and state the
hypothesis in E2 as "the prefill wait tracks lambda E[S^2]/(2(1-rho))",
which is what Prop. 1 prices. State plainly that at the observed reuse
the append is at least half of Var[S] and that the mixture becomes the
larger source only once eviction is active (Table 9's 38 to 75% is the
simulated version of that claim, and should be cited as such).

### M7. §4.1 over-claims in places and under-reads its own tables

Where: §4.1 paragraphs "Open sessions and admission", "Placement and a
shared KV store", "Replayed production sessions"; Table 8; Table 9;
Table 6.

(a) Table 6/7: "Priced/tau" uses tau_i = the class mean tool time and
q_i = the class resume probability, i.e. the generating distribution.
The priced rules are oracle policies compared with blind ones; say so in
the caption and in the text, and note that E4 uses estimates.
(b) Table 8: the sentence "The utilisation of the affinity node at the
inversion follows rho^* for cheap moves and exceeds it for the expensive
one, because the link is itself a shared resource" is not supported by
the table. At B = 500k the observed rho_0 = 0.19 is below rho^* = 0.25,
not "following" it; at B = 20000k and 2000k the inversion is below the
lowest load swept (0.13), so nothing is learned; at B = 125k the
inversion is found between rates 0.3 and 1.6 with rho_0 = 1.00, which
means the affinity node was saturated at the first rate that resolved
it, so the "exceeds rho^*" reading is a resolution artefact until the
sweep is refined between 0.3 and 1.6. The mechanistic explanation (link
saturation) is plausible but is asserted, not shown; add the link
utilisation as a column or drop the sentence. The row order "is the
proposition's" claim is fine.
(c) Table 9: see M2 for the closed-system reading. In addition, sessions
were formed by splitting the corpus at gaps > 600 s (183 sessions become
761), so every split starts a "session" with a cold prefill of the whole
accumulated context (mean final context 391k). The 5 to 11 s open TTFT
"because a new session's first prompt, of a few hundred thousand tokens,
is a cold prefill" is therefore partly an artefact of the split rule, and
the paper should report the first-turn context distribution of the
replayed sessions and justify 600 s (or replay with the store keeping
KV across gaps below a threshold).
(d) Table 5, "arbitrary w_i" row: SF's ratio of 6.7e6 is meaningless (SF
does not see w_i); either drop the cell or say it is the price of
ignoring w_i entirely.
(e) The section is 4.5 pages of an 8-page paper for results the paper
calls "illustrative". Keep Table 2 (in-model, condensed with Table 3),
Table 7 + Figure 3 (admission), Table 9 (replay), and Figure 2 (LPS).
Move Tables 3, 4, 5, 6, 8 and Figure 1 to an appendix; the text already
says what they show.

### M8. §4.2 "What the traces say": the proxy TTFT paragraph is close to responsible but two sentences overreach

Where: §4.2, "The proxy's TTFT tells a different story ... On this
production stack, at a hit rate near one, TTFT is set by something the
trace does not record, presumably the queue and the cache tier, which is
what E2 controls for."

What is right: the R^2 = 0.01 observation is reported with its limits,
the caption says the serving stack is unknown, and AGENTS.md rule 7's
"observational" status is respected in the last sentence.

What is wrong: (a) "at a hit rate near one" is a property of a
never-evicting counterfactual cache computed from the hashes, not of the
production system; the production hit rate is unobserved and the paper
must not assert it. (b) "presumably the queue and the cache tier" is a
guess dressed as an inference; a TTFT with CV^2 = 1.0, median 3.5 s,
p90 8.2 s, and a slightly negative log-log slope on the append is equally
consistent with a fixed per-request overhead (API layers, proxy,
routing) that has nothing to do with queueing. The paragraph currently
reads as motivation for the paper's thesis; it is not evidence for it.
(c) The paragraph does not draw the one conclusion the data does
support and that the paper's own replay found: in an all-hit regime the
prefill wait is dominated by cold first prompts and by misses, not by
appends, which is exactly why the append's R^2 is near zero if the
model is right. That is a prediction E2 can test, and should be phrased
as such.

Fix: rewrite the two sentences as: "Under a never-evicting cache 98.8%
of these tokens would hit; the production cache and load are
unobserved. TTFT at the proxy is not explained by the append (R^2 =
0.01) or the context (0.06); it has CV^2 = 1.0. The model predicts this
in an all-hit regime, where the prefill wait is set by cold first
prompts and misses (Table 9), and E2 tests that prediction by observing
the queue." Move Table 10's TTFT half to the appendix; keep the workload
half in the main text.

### M9. Related work: positioning is fair but the novelty sentence is missing, and the closest prior work is unverified

Where: §5, paper/refs.bib notes.

What is wrong: (a) The economics-of-queues sentence ("there the price
steers customers who decide whether to join, whereas here it ranks the
states a server keeps") undersells the overlap: the marginal externality
of a job of size x at an M/G/1 FIFO queue, with its x^2 term, is the
content of Mendelson and Whang (1990) and Dewan and Mendelson (1990);
Prop. 1 is that externality applied to a change of job size. Say so,
and claim the application (KV miss, hit/miss mixture, the stage
separation), not the formula. Both entries are marked UNVERIFIED; read
them before submission. (b) Continuum sets a TTL "from the reload cost
and the queueing delay an eviction would induce", which is a price of a
miss with two of the three terms; the paper says "a heuristic form of
§2.5" and should say precisely which term Continuum lacks (the
head-of-line term and the load factor) and that this is an abstract-only
reading. (c) InferCept is described "to our understanding" from memory
(bib: UNVERIFIED); it is the closest systems work and the description
must be checked. (d) KVLearn "learns a retention score that weighs
recompute, transfer and storage costs and adapts its threshold from
observed hit rates and memory pressure": the paper's theta and u_i are
the analytical counterpart; the paper should say what the analysis adds
(the ratio bounds, the special-case reading), since a learned score with
the same inputs would otherwise dominate. (e) Ao et al. 2026: the
thrashing finding of §4.1 is acknowledged as the same phenomenon; then
§4.1 and the abstract should not present "memory thrashing, not eviction
order, decides throughput" as a finding without that attribution. (f)
Mitzenmacher and Shahout is cited for the agenda; their TRAIL
(shahout2024trail) memory-aware SRPT is in the same M/G/1 world and the
paper should say why a prefill queue that serves in order (no SRPT) is
the right model when Sarathi-style schedulers could reorder prefills.
(g) 25 entries are cited from abstracts only (research-plan §6). That is
acceptable for a draft and unacceptable for submission; the Related
Work section makes specific claims ("prove NP-hardness", "give the first
constant-competitive") that need the full text.

## Minor issues

- Abstract: "The price is exact to first order" is right; "unbounded near
  capacity" is the PK pole and reads as a result. Replace with what
  Prop. 1(ii) actually says: which term dominates at which load.
- Abstract and §1 say "an uncalibrated simulation ... finds that memory
  thrashing, not eviction order, decides throughput"; §4.1 says this
  only for the two-class synthetic scenario and the replay. Say
  "in simulation" in the abstract.
- Table 1, "Sessions": "after a tool call of mean tau_i it resumes with
  probability p_i" vs §2.4 "expected further tau_i seconds". Choose the
  remaining time (that is what pricing needs).
- Eq. (2): "bytes-worth of time", beta "KV bytes per token"; §4.1 uses
  beta in seconds per token. One unit.
- §2.2 "the schedulers we know of serve pending prefills in arrival
  order": vLLM's scheduler has priority and preemption knobs, and
  Sarathi's chunking does interleave two prefills' chunks in some
  configurations. Hedge to "by default".
- Prop. 1: "Let rho' = rho + lambda sum q_i Delta S_i < 1" belongs in the
  hypotheses, not after "and let".
- Prop. 4(ii) wording: "discard e and every program with w_j > w_e, and
  evict e together with the remaining programs". "Discard" and "evict"
  read as synonyms. Say "exclude from the pool".
- Prop. 5(ii): "frees Delta C whenever the pool holds it" is trivial for
  any order that stops on reaching the target; drop it from the
  statement.
- §3.2 Eq. (9): the `\provedby` sits after a displayed equation with no
  claim in prose; the equivalence is definitional. Fine to keep, but say
  "by definition" in the sentence.
- §3 "The inputs are observable": p_i and tau_i are estimates from the
  tool and the phase; call them estimates here as the Limitations does.
- §4.1 "Of 34 checks, 34 pass": research-plan.md says 31 of 31 and
  simulation-design.md says 22 checks. Internal, but sync the docs.
- Table 6 caption: "Priced: q_i Phi_i/c_i" uses q_i for the resume
  probability that the text calls p_i. One symbol.
- Table 7 caption states 95% half-widths are in the figure; the figure

## Questions to the authors

1. What is the first-turn context distribution of the 761 replayed
   sessions, and how much of Table 9's open-pool TTFT (5 to 11 s) comes
   from the 600 s split rule rather than from genuinely new sessions?
2. In Table 9's tight-cap cells the entry queue grows for the whole
   20 000 s horizon. Are Hit, Mix, CV^2 and W_q time averages over a
   transient, and do they change with the horizon?
3. What is the measured interarrival CV^2 of turns at the prefill queue
   in the replay? §2.2 invokes Kingman (bursty arrivals add delay); §4.1
   finds the opposite sign. Which effect wins in which regime, and can
   the two be separated by reporting c_a^2 next to N?
4. For the Priced rules in Tables 6 and 7, are p_i and tau_i the class
   parameters (oracle) or estimated online? If oracle, what happens with
   estimates from a running mean of resume events?
5. In the inversion sweep, what were the rates between 0.3 and 1.6, and
   what was the link utilisation at the inversion for B = 125k?
6. Does the paper's u_i-threshold (byte-seconds) have an optimality
   statement of its own, or is Prop. 3 (bytes) being cited for it?
7. The trace corpus gives per-session turn counts and gaps. Have you
   estimated p_i (probability of another turn given turn index and gap)
   and its spread? Prop. 5(i)'s relevance and the E4 hypothesis both rest
   on that spread being large, and the data to check it is in hand.
8. What is the target venue if E1 to E6 are not run before the deadline?
   The current text cannot be submitted to ICML with §4.2 in the main
   body; a theory-plus-simulation paper needs M2 and M3 resolved, a
   systems paper needs the measurements.
