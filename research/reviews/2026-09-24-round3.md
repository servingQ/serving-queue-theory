## Verdict

**Minor revision, on the border of major.** v0.9 does what round 2
asked: the finite-population statement is now a proposition whose Lean
statement matches the prose line by line, the M/M/1//N table is in the
paper, the forced-miss numbers are generated, and the residual
mismatches of round 1 (definitions, proofs, citations) are down to a
handful of sentences. None of the issues below needs new science. Two
of them, however, must be fixed before this can be submitted anywhere:
the paper is 1.1 pages over the limit, and the forced-miss experiment
that carries the abstract's last clause runs in a different
configuration from the one its caption and prose describe, and is read
in a way its own numbers do not support.

**Single biggest remaining weakness.** The paper's central quantity is
still the open-queue price, its own evidence says that price is off by
1.3 to 7 times in the regime the paper says matters (5 to 12 live
sessions), and Proposition 2 bounds only the direction of the error.
Table 13, the one experiment that measures the error, was run with no
cap on live sessions (`base.max_sessions = None`) while its caption
says "at most 24 live" and the prose explains its delta = 10 % rows by
"the closed system saturates gracefully". The finite-population reading
of the replay is right in mechanism and wrong in the details that a
reviewer will check first.

## Remaining major issues (most severe first)

### 1. Table 13 is not the experiment the paper describes, and its delta = 10 % rows are misread

Where: Table 13 caption ("with no pool limit (open, Poisson sessions at
rate Lambda per s, at most 24 live, the configuration of the first rows
of Table 5)"); §4.2 ("at delta = 10 % the observed rise is 1.2 to 3.4
times the lower end and the upper end diverges, because the added load
puts the open queue near its pole while the closed system saturates
gracefully, and because the misses of long-context sessions lengthen the
sessions themselves, which the stationary price does not count. The
open price is therefore a bound for a few misses and not for many");
abstract ("the open price overstates the cost of a few misses
several-fold and understates that of many"); §6 ("a conjecture that the
replay supports for a few misses and refutes for many").

What is wrong, in order:

(a) *Configuration.* `trace_price_row` (validation.rs) builds the
baseline with `trace_replay_cfg(..., TRACE_CAP_OPEN, ...)` and then sets
`base.max_sessions = None;`, so neither the baseline nor the forced run
caps live sessions. The caption's "at most 24 live" and "the
configuration of the first rows of Table 5" are false, and the table
proves it: at (Lambda, delta) = (0.01, 0.10) the rise is Delta L_P =
135.4 over a baseline L_P of 3.5 (trace.csv, `prefill_number`), so about
139 turns are in the prefill stage on average, which needs at least 139
live sessions. With a cap of 24, Prop. 2(iii) would bound the rise by
24 - 3.5 = 20.5.

(b) *Stationarity.* hi = ∞ in two cells means rho' ≥ 1 for the open
model, i.e. the forced misses add more prefill work than the stage has
capacity for. With no cap on sessions the simulated system is then
itself overloaded: the live population and the prefill queue grow over
the horizon (66 000 s), and the reported time averages (35.1 and 135.4)
depend on the horizon, like the entry waits that Table 10's caption
already flags as horizon-dependent. The seed half-widths (± 4.9, ± 15.8)
do not reveal this. The forced run's live count is computed by the
simulator (`r1.mean_sessions`) and not recorded; the table shows only
the baseline's N-bar.

(c) *Reading.* The delta = 10 % rows do not refute the bracket of Prop.
1: 20.3 lies in [17.2, 647.1] and 135.4 lies in [39.8, ∞]. What they
show is that the *lower end* understates by 1.2 to 3.4 times when
(1 - rho)/(1 - rho') is large or infinite, which is exactly the factor
Prop. 1 states. So "understates the cost of many" is not a
finite-population finding and not a failure of the open price; it is
the open price's own load factor. The finite-population finding is the
small-delta rows (0.15 to 0.75 of the lower end, i.e. below the bracket),
and it is a real one.

(d) *The conjecture.* §6 says the replay "refutes for many" the
M/G/1//N form of Prop. 2(ii), the statement W_N ≤ open wait at the same
utilisation. Table 13 does not test that inequality; Table 5 does, and
supports it in all eight cells (PK/W_q from 4 to 17). Nothing in the
paper refutes the conjecture.

(e) *The explanation.* "while the closed system saturates gracefully"
describes a system that was not simulated (no cap), and "the misses of
long-context sessions lengthen the sessions themselves" is offered
without the one number that would support it (N-bar under the forced
run). In an open system with fixed Lambda the turn rate is unchanged in
steady state; longer sessions mean more live sessions, which is a
restatement of Little's law, not an effect the price misses.

Fix (one simulator run plus rewriting):

1. Run Table 13 with `max_sessions = Some(TRACE_CAP_OPEN)` (or a cap
   equal to the baseline's rounded N-bar, 5 and 12, which makes the
   population genuinely the one the prose talks about). Add columns:
   N-bar under the forced run, rho' (the open model's load after the
   misses), and, where rho' ≥ 1, print hi as "∞ (rho' ≥ 1)". With the
   cap, Prop. 2(iii) gives a finite bound in every cell and both hi = ∞
   rows become tests of "min(hi, N - L_P)" rather than of the horizon.
2. §4.2: "At delta ≤ 3 %, with [macro] to [macro] sessions live, the
   observed rise is [macro] to [macro] of the bracket's lower end, so
   the open price overstates the cost of a few misses, as the finite
   population predicts (Prop. 2). At delta = 10 % the added work puts
   the open model at rho' ≥ [macro]; its lower end understates the rise
   by [macro] to [macro] times and its upper end is infinite in [n]
   cells, so the bracket holds and only its load factor is informative,
   while the capped replica's rise stays below N - L_P."
3. Abstract: "the open price overstates the cost of a few misses
   several-fold, and for many misses only its load-corrected upper end
   brackets the rise" (or: "and understates it only through the load
   factor Proposition 1 names").
4. §6: replace "a conjecture that the replay supports for a few misses
   and refutes for many" by "a conjecture that the replay supports in
   every cell of Table 5 (the open wait exceeds the observed one by 4
   to 17 times)".
5. Delete the two "because" clauses or replace them with the observed
   N-bar under the forced run.

### 3. Proposition 2: statement honest, three sentences around it are not

(a) *Part (iii) is one-sided and content-light.* `closed_price_cap`
proves L' - L ≤ N - L from L' ≤ N; the paper says "changes the mean
number in the prefill stage by at most N - L_P", which reads as
|Delta L_P| ≤ N - L_P and is false when L_P > N/2 (a policy can lower
L_P by up to L_P). §1 has the same slip: "no policy change moves the
mean number in the stage by more than N - L_P". Write "raises" in both
places. Since the part is a one-line consequence of "at most N turns
exist", the house rule (demote near-trivial results to a prose
sentence with an inline `\provedby`, keep the Lean theorem) applies; I
suggested it as a part in round 2 and, seeing it typeset, I withdraw
that: make it the sentence after the proposition. Note also that (iii)
needs no exponential assumption; say so if it stays.

(b) *"The usable price of a miss at a replica with N live sessions is
therefore the smaller of the bracket and N - L_P"* (§2.4, repeated in
§6 and in the research plan). Two problems. The bracket bounds the
aggregate rise Delta L_P, not a price per miss, so "price" and
"N - L_P" are in different units unless "bracket" means the aggregate
[lo, hi]. And the bracket is a theorem about the open M/G/1; for the
finite-source replica nothing proves Delta L_P ≤ hi, so the minimum is
a valid bound only through N - L_P, and Table 13 shows the minimum
still overstates a few misses by 1.3 to 7 times. Write: "so a policy
change at a replica with N live sessions raises L_P by at most
min(hi, N - L_P), and for a few misses even that cap overstates the
rise several-fold (Table 13)." Drop "therefore".

(c) *"Part (ii) says that the price of Proposition 1, which uses the
open wait, does not understate the Markovian finite-source wait."* A
price is not a wait. Part (ii) says the open wait W that enters (4)
does not understate the finite-source wait; whether the *price* built
from it under- or overstates the finite-source *rise* is exactly what
Table 13 measures and (i)–(iii) do not settle. Say "the wait W that
enters the price of Proposition 1".

(d) *Hypotheses and provenance.* The recursion is asserted in the
statement ("given exactly by") and the proof says "by the arrival
theorem"; neither the statement nor Appendix A cites mean value
analysis (Reiser and Lavenberg 1980, or Lazowska et al. 1984 §6, which
is already in the bibliography). The Lean development takes the
recursion as the model and proves the inequalities from it; the arrival
theorem is cited, not formalised. That is fine under AGENTS.md rule 1
(established result in prose with a citation) but the Appendix A
preamble should say so in one clause, since it currently says "Every
proposition ... has also been formalised". Also, exponential think time
is not needed: the delay station is insensitive, so the same recursion
is exact for any think-time law with mean Z (BCMP), which matters here
because the observed think time has CV^2 = 304 (Table 3). One clause
after the statement ("the think-time law enters only through Z") would
pre-empt a reviewer who knows this.

(e) *Every appearance of the bound carries its hypothesis.* Checked:
abstract ("its Markovian form"), §1 ("for its Markovian form"), §2.2
("bounds the Markovian wait"), §2.4 (explicit), §4.2 ("for exponential
work"), §6 ("proved for exponential work only"), §5 ("the
finite-population bound", no hypothesis, acceptable in a list of
claims). Adequate. §6 should read "exponential work (and a mean think
time)" once (d) is done.
